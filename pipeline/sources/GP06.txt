I.          General Permit Applicability

I.A.        This General Permit, GP06, can be used only for a diesel fuel-fired reciprocating
            internal combustion engine (RICE) that is a stationary source, including a
            portable unit which meets the following requirements:

            I.A.1.    It is a true minor or synthetic minor for Operating Permits (OP),
                      Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD) or New Source Review
                      (NSR) programs applicability, except as provided for in I.A.5. and
                      I.A.6. below.

            I.A.2.    It is a true minor or synthetic minor under the provisions of 40 CFR
                      Part 63, National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants for
                      Source Categories.

            I.A.3.    It meets all the requirements in New Source Performance Standard
                      (NSPS) Subpart IIII as applicable to the specific engine, including the
                      standards required for relocating into Colorado as set in Regulation
                      Number 6, Part B, Section I.C.

            I.A.4.    It meets all of the conditions of Section II.A of this General Permit,
                      GP06.

     I.B.    Stationary Sources that became subject to Title V Operating Permit
             requirements or became classified as an existing major stationary source for
             which a complete GP06 registration request was received by the Division prior
             to the effective date (January 27, 2020) on which the 8-hour Ozone Control
             Area was re-classified from moderate to serious may continue to operate
             under this General Permit. 8-hour Ozone Control Area is as defined in




                                           Page 3 of 35
         Regulation Number 7, Part A, Section II.A.1. (Adopted 12/14/2023, Effective
         02/14/2024).

 I.C.    Stationary Sources that: a) became subject to Title V Operating Permit
         requirements or became classified as an existing major stationary source due
         to the reclassification of the 8-hour Ozone Control Area from serious to
         severe, and b) for which a complete GP06 registration request was received
         by the Division prior to the effective date of GP06, Issuance 3, (November 3,
         2022), may continue to operate under this General Permit. 8-hour Ozone
         Control Area is as defined in Regulation Number 7, Part A, Section II.A.1.
         (Adopted 12/14/2023, Effective 02/14/2024).

 I.D.    Stationary Sources that: a) became subject to Title V Operating Permit
         requirements or became classified as an existing major stationary source for
         which a GP06 registration request was deemed complete by the Division prior
         to the effective date (July 24, 2024) on which Northern Weld County was re-
         classified from moderate to serious may continue to operate under this
         General Permit. Northern Weld County is as defined in Regulation Number 7,
         Part A, Section II.A.3. (Adopted 12/14/2023, Effective 02/14/2024).

I.E.    The following types of sources may not use this General Permit for diesel fuel
        fired reciprocating internal combustion engines:

         I.E.1.    Any diesel fuel-fired internal combustion engine that does not meet
                   the requirements of NSPS Subpart IIII as applicable to the specific
                   engine.

         I.E.2.    Newly constructed or modified existing equipment that is part of a
                   project permit action that is subject to Non-Attainment New Source



                                      Page 4 of 35
                    Review (NA NSR) or Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD)
                    permitting under Regulation Number 3, Part D.

I.F.    This General Permit applies only to the equipment as described in Section I.A
        of this General Permit above for which the operator has obtained approval
        from the Division for registration under this General Permit, GP06. Other
        equipment at the same stationary source must be permitted separately as
        required by Regulation Number 3, Part B.

I.G.    For new or modified registrations deemed complete on or after the effective
        date of this permit issuance (Issuance 4), the maximum allowable emissions of
        nitrogen oxides (NOx), carbon monoxide (CO), and volatile organic compounds
        (VOC) from all diesel-fueled engines under the GP06 registration that are
        owned and operated by the same person and co-located at the same stationary
        source must not exceed the requested Federally-enforceable annual emission
        limitations (tons per year) as listed on the general permit registration approval
        letter, or if an approval letter has not been issued, as certified by the owner or
        operator in the Air Pollutant Emission Notice (APEN) included with the general

        II.A.7.)

III.E. NSPS Units:

    III.E.1.       This engine must comply with the New Source Performance Standards
                   (NSPS) requirements of Regulation Number 6, Part A, Subpart IIII,
                   Standards of Performance for Stationary Compression Ignition Internal
                   Combustion Engines (CI ICE). This includes all the applicable
                   requirements including, but not limited to, the following:

               III.E.1.a.     All fuel used shall meet the following specifications:

                       III.E.1.a.(i)   Sulfur content shall not exceed 15 ppm.

       III.E.1.a.(ii) Have a minimum cetane index of 40 or have a
                    maximum aromatic compound content of 35% by
                    volume.

       III.E.1.a.(iii) Compliance shall be demonstrated by maintaining
                    copies of the fuel specifications provided by the
                    supplier on-site or in a readily accessible location
                    and made available to the Division for inspection
                    upon request.

III.E.1.b.   The engine and control devices must be installed,
             configured, operated, and maintained according to the
             specifications and instructions provided by the engine
             manufacturer.

III.E.1.c.   If the engine is equipped with a diesel particulate filter, the
             filter must be installed with a backpressure monitor that
             notifies the owner or operator when the high backpressure
             limit of the engine is approached. Records shall be kept of
             any corrective action taken after the backpressure monitor
             has notified the owner or operator that the high
             backpressure limit is approached.

III.E.1.d.   If the engine is used for emergency purposes, a non-
             resettable hour meter must be installed prior to start-up.

III.E.1.e.   If the diesel fuel-fired engine covered under this general
             permit avoids tier 4 or 4i requirements by being an
             emergency generator, the engine shall not be used for any
             purpose except emergency power generation and for the


                       Page 14 of 35
                    purpose of maintenance checks and readiness testing,
                    provided that the tests are recommended by Federal, State
                    or local government, the manufacturer, the vendor, or the
                    insurance company associated with the engine.
                    Maintenance checks and readiness testing of such units is
                    limited to 100 hours per year. There is no time limit on the
                    use of emergency stationary ICE in emergency situations.
                    The owner or operator may petition the Administrator for
                    approval of additional hours to be used for maintenance
                    checks and readiness testing, but a petition is not required
                    if the owner or operator maintains records indicating that
                    Federal, State, or local standards require maintenance and
                    testing of emergency ICE beyond 100 hours per year.
                    Emergency stationary ICE may operate up to 50 hours per
                    year in non- emergency situations, but those 50 hours are
                    counted towards the 100 hours per year provided for
                    maintenance and testing. The 50 hours per year for non-
                    emergency situations cannot be used for peak shaving or to
                    generate income for a facility to supply power to an
                    electric grid or otherwise supply non-emergency power as
                    part of a financial arrangement with another entity. For
                    owners and operators of emergency engines, any operation
                    other than emergency operation, maintenance and testing,
                    and operation in non- emergency situations for 50 hours per
                    year, as permitted in this section, is prohibited.

X.     Engine Alternative Operating Scenarios (AOS)

X.A.   Provided that the applicability requirements of Section I and emission limits set
       forth in Section II are still met, the permittee may invoke an AOS for the
       following modifications to an existing engine registered under the general
       permit without modifying the general permit registration.

       X.A.1.      An existing registered engine may be temporarily replaced with a
                   different or like-kind engine in accordance with the most recent
                   version of PS Memo 11-04 for “Stationary Compression Ignition (CI)
                   Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) AOS”.

                X.A.1.a.     A temporary replacement is defined in this permit as in the
                             same service for 270 operating days or less in any 12-month
                             period. The 270 days is the total number of days that the
                             equipment is in operation. If the equipment operates only
                             part of a day, that day counts towards the 270-day total.

       X.A.2.      Routine maintenance, repair, or like-kind replacement of control
                   equipment

       X.A.3.      Addition of a control device where the operator will not claim
                   Federally enforceable credit for the emission reductions achieved.

X.B.   A like-kind replacement is defined as replacing a piece of equipment with the
       same make, model, and design capacity as the original piece of equipment.
