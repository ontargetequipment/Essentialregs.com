May 2026 update: Electronic air quality forms

The CDPHE Air Pollution Control Division is continuing its transition to electronic forms in 2026. Visit the electronic submission page to determine if the form you need is available electronically. If the electronic form is not listed, complete and submit the PDF version via email or mail.

Starting June 30, 2026, all applicants must use the online submission tool for available forms, including Air Pollutant Emission Notices (APENs) and construction permit applications. This means applicants must submit all new APEN and construction permit applications using the online tool. All Title V (major source) operators applying for a construction permit must also use the online tool.

On June 1, the division will remove applicable PDF versions of air forms from its web pages. Applicants must use the online submission tool starting June 30 for applicable air form submissions, including for APENs and construction permits.

The division’s online submission tool now includes electronic forms for: 

  • All APENs.
    • Note: The Facility-wide Greenhouse Gas Emissions APEN (Form APCD-235) will remain open for PDF submission until further notice.
  • All construction permit applications.
  • Other required forms, including:
    • Environmental Justice Summary.
    • Modeling Requirements Determination (Form APCD-114).
    • Notice of Startup.
    • Relocation Notice.

The division anticipates launching an electronic submission process for Title V operating permit applications by the end of 2026. The process will use the same online submission tool currently available for APEN and construction permit applications.

Overview

This page provides information for air permit applicants about general permits, forms, and processes.

General air permits offer a streamlined approach to permitting. They're available for:

  • Certain oil and gas operations. 
  • Land development activities.

Sources may qualify for a general permit if they can meet the criteria of emissions limitations and other operating conditions.

When the general permit isn't applicable or doesn't meet a facility's needs, the CDPHE Air Pollution Control Division issues an individual permit through the traditional construction permit process.

Environmental justice and modeling determination requirements

You must submit any required Environmental Justice Summary and Modeling Determination forms before you submit a permit application. A complete Environmental Justice Summary is required for most new or modified air permits.

If a Modeling Determination is also required, submit your modeling determination request (APCD Form-114) soon after you receive the Environmental Justice Summary confirmation number. Effective August 2023, sources with any quantifiable amount of new emissions of nitrogen oxides (NOx), sulfur dioxide (SO2), particulate matter (PM), or carbon monoxide (CO) must include a completed Modeling Determination (Form APCD-114) in a general permit registration, unless otherwise exempted under section 2.4 of the Permitting Section Addendum to the Modeling Guideline (Feb.15, 2024).

Sources newly seeking coverage under a general permit must submit a demonstration of compliance with the National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) with the general permit registration. Refer to the air quality modeling guidance for permits page for more information.

If a modeling determination is required, applicants must:

  • Submit the Form APCD-114 to the division.
  • Obtain a modeling determination from the division. 
  • Provide the division’s determination with the general permit application.

If a modeling analysis is required, applicants must:

  • Submit the Model Analysis and all associated files to the division for review.
  • Obtain a completeness determination from the division.
  • Obtain a final Model Review Report from the division.
  • Provide the final Model Review Report with the general permit application.
    • For more information on requirements for submitting a model analysis, review the Colorado Minor NSR Source Modeling Guideline for Air Quality Permits, Appendix C (March 1, 2025).

If an applicant does not receive notification of a completeness determination from the division within 60 days of the division's receipt of the general permit registration, the registration will be deemed complete. 

  • Reference the "General Permit Registration Approval Process" terms within each general permit, which require a complete registration before starting construction.
  • More guidance for the APCD Form-114 and modeling analysis process is available on the air quality modeling guidance for permits page.

General Permit 12 for oil and gas operations

The division is accepting applications as of July 15, 2026 for the General Permit 12 (GP12). The division published the GP12 in May 2026. Applicants must use the electronic submission tool for GP12.

Learn more about GP12.

The GP12 replaces GP09 and GP10 as the only facility-wide general permit for constructing and operating new well production facilities. GP09 and GP10 applications submitted before July 15, 2026 will remain active. Air pollution sources constructed and operating under GP09 and GP10 will maintain coverage until they expire, and the division will grant extensions as outlined in Permitting Section Memo 20-03. The GP12 does not follow the same expiration date as GP09 and GP10.

The final GP12 provides a voluntary mechanism for companies to obtain an air permit to authorize the construction of well production facilities. It is intended to provide an enhanced alternative to the existing general permits. The GP12 applies to equipment, including but not limited to:

  • Diesel-fired internal combustion engines.
  • Fugitive component leak emissions.
  • Gas venting from separators.
  • Hydrocarbon liquids loadout operations.
  • Natural gas-fired internal combustion engines.
  • Routine or predictable emissions (ROPE).
  • Storage tanks.

The Permitting Section Memo 26-01 provides more information about the GP12. Applicants must submit and obtain an approved Form APCD-114 or an ambient air impact analysis to show compliance with National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS).

Alternatively, operators that apply for GP12 will have an additional way to demonstrate compliance with the NAAQS through the  GP12 modeling workbooks. The workbooks are specifically designed for new well production facilities seeking coverage under the GP12 to construct and operate. Operators that need to complete modeling requirements can review the workbooks to apply for a GP12. Operators that apply for GP12 using a GP12 modeling workbook must submit and obtain an approved GP12 modeling workbook. Email completed workbooks to cdphe_apcd_modeling_files@state.co.us.

Learn more about GP12 workbooks.

General permit updates and revisions

For permits below, you must submit the form using the electronic submission tool. For more information, visit the electronic submission frequently asked questions web page.

In July 2025, the division published revisions to several general permits that apply primarily to oil and gas operators in Colorado. In September 2025, the division published a guidance document that covers frequently asked questions on the revised general permits and registration process. The revised general permits include:

  • GP01: Condensate Storage Tank Batteries.
  • GP02: Natural Gas Fired Reciprocating Internal Combustion Engines.
  • GP05: Produced Water Storage Tank Batteries.
  • GP06: Diesel Engines.
  • GP07: Hydrocarbon Liquid Loadout.
  • GP08: Oil and Gas Industry Storage Tanks.
  • GP09: Oil and Gas Well Production Facilities.
  • GP10: Oil and Gas Well Production Facilities.
  • GP11: Oil and Gas Industry Routine or Predictable Gas Venting Emissions.

The revisions include updates to address the following:

  • Reclassification of the ground-level ozone nonattainment areas.
  • New regulations and updates to the Air Quality Control Commission Regulations 3, 7, and 26.
  • Consistent language and formatting for general permits and division policy, such as monitoring frequencies and facility-wide emission limitations.
  • Requirements from Regulation 3, Part B, III.D.1, as they relate to NAAQS and Alternative Operating Scenarios (AOS).
  • Registration approval process clarifications.

The division developed proposed revisions in 2024 and provided a 30-day public comment period for review and written feedback. The division considered feedback before publishing the final revisions in July 2025.
